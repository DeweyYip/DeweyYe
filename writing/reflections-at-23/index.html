I Exist, Therefore I Am

19 November 2026

I exist.

But this life can be easily taken away.

An accident, an illness you never saw coming, or someone who just lost his mind and wants to stab you to death when you're just walking down the street could easily end it.

Life is as brittle as a cracker. And I don't think people talk about this side of life enough.

I've had a few experiences that made me confront the possibility of death. The most recent one happened while I was writing this. I was potentially exposed to rabies, got vaccinated, and have been following medical advice for a few days.

Death now feels personal. I felt numb, mostly. It was certainly unfair, but I was strangely calm about it at first. I think I’m a good person, believe it or not. But being a good person doesn’t protect me from a horrific death.

And I tell myself, none of us gets to take anything with us in the end. Sometimes when I look ahead at my life, I know I'll achieve something. But after that, I'm gonna die anyways.

And then I started to think, will it be painful? I'm certainly scared of it. A wave of sadness hit me. I couldn't remain calm no more.

If I have to experience this moment, why would I even need to live? I can't calmly face death if I don't figure out life at this point.

It felt strange to me when I turned 23 last month. And all these experiences made me realize that, in my 23 years of being alive, I'd never seriously considered what it means to be alive.

Once I started thinking about it, so many questions suddenly felt trivial. Sometimes, hearing people talk so confidently about how everyone should live makes me think, who gives an F? UR GONNA DIE. Sooner or later… lol.

I don't particularly like this mindset. But I guess I've been experiencing some existential anxiety lately. And the possibility of dying without ever understanding life feels unfashionably anticlimactic.

What makes a life meaningful? And what makes it worth living?

I haven't come across any explanation that settles these questions perfectly. Can’t trust those larpers who act like they've figured it all out. Especially when their beautiful philosophy is nowhere to be found in how they actually live.

Money Is The Anthem

The familiar story goes: money can't buy happiness, fame is superficial, and love is what truly matters. ☝️🤓

Unpopular opinion: Why should love be inherently meaningful while ambition is inherently shallow ???

This sounds hypocritical to me anyways. Both are common human desires that can profoundly shape our experiences. That doesn't make them equally meaningful, but why should one automatically be considered profound while the other is dismissed as shallow?

If someone genuinely finds fulfillment in becoming wealthy and being recognized, I don't see how that alone makes their life meaningless.

And if I'm being completely honest here, I have some pretty straightforward aspirations.

Like, multiple houses in Malibu with those big ass pools. More cars. More watches. First class always. Fancier and fancier stuff I might not even need so I put it in my closet for showing off. 😭🥺 Of course I also want to be a badass engineer, possibly a scientist, but babe, these things don’t exclude each other.

And let's say I have two lives to choose from.

In the first, I'm like crazy rich and famous, deeply loved by my family and friends, and genuinely happy. In the second, I have a modest income, basically an NPC, but I'm equally happy and equally loved.

I think I'd choose without hesitation.

Because why would I choose the second one? Apparently, there's something I want from the first life. Maybe status, maybe freedom, maybe just the satisfaction of having more. That doesn't necessarily make it the better life. It just makes it the one I'd choose for myself.

I don't lack love and that spiritual stuff, I promise, and I'm not an ungrateful motherfucker. At least I think so? But I genuinely believe my life could be better off with a stupid amount of sneakers and watches.

Lol, plot twist. I know nobody gets to take their cash with them when they die. This part is true. But why should that automatically make a materialistic life meaningless? And why should the inevitability of death reduce the significance of what we experience while we're alive?

And there's another question. What's the point of always asking what something is for? Asking if something matters to me matters, and if the question of asking whether something matters to me matters matters. What's the point ????

We humans are so accustomed to doing things for something else. What if there's no further purpose? Maybe happiness, love, curiosity, achievement, and experiences can be valued for their own sake.

Still, just because something matters to me doesn't necessarily mean it's meaningful.

So who actually gets to decide?

Who Gets to Decide?

Maybe one way to approach meaning is through purpose.

We create chairs for sitting. That's their intended purpose. But can we apply the same understanding of purpose to human existence?

And if we have a creator, would that creator determine the meaning of our lives? I don't wanna go down contemplating God here, but let's say that God exists, created humanity, and had a particular purpose in mind.

But would that necessarily be the only meaning our lives could have?

Back to the chair, someone else might think, well, that's a hell of a chair! and put it in a museum. Then the chair has a significance beyond its original use.

Human life doesn't work like a chair. But it illustrates a possibility: the purpose for which something was created might not exhaust everything it can mean.

And I see no reason to automatically assume that society gets the final say either.

Or is it our own subjective feelings? Can I just determine the meaning of my own life?

Meaning, Meaning, Meaning...

If someone genuinely believes their life is meaningless, does that make it so? Maybe it means their life feels meaningless to them. But does that necessarily mean there's nothing else that could make their existence meaningful?

Not a big fan of this subjectivist notion to be fair. Especially when someone is struggling with depression, I wouldn't want to treat their judgment of themselves as a final verdict on their existence. If I can be mistaken about the meaninglessness of my own life, I can also be mistaken about its meaningfulness.

Think of different ways of living:

A life devoted to serving God.

A life devoted to chasing paper.

A life spent doomscrolling.

A soldier's life cut short by war.

Can we really say that all these lives are equally meaningful?

I don't know…

They may differ a lot in their consequences, experiences, and the significance they hold for those who live them and those around them. The absence of a universally established ranking doesn't mean they must all be equal.

But I'm not ready to hand that judgment over to society, religion, or some hypothetical cosmic authority either.

So who the heck gets to decide? 😂

Perhaps there's no ultimate authority. Perhaps there are many kinds of meaning. Or perhaps none of our lives has any ultimate meaning at all.

Rationally, I can accept the possibility that life has no predetermined purpose. Psychologically, though, that uncertainty still bothers me.

And the more I ask, the more questions I seem to create. 😡

And maybe the fact that I want an answer doesn't mean an answer must exist.

Perhaps we can simply be the authors of our lives without constantly asking who gets the final say on what they mean.

And at this moment, I'll stop asking why and why and more whys, because I don't need to figure it out today to live this life.

One More Question...

Cool, enough about meaning. But what about value?

Does value come from our subjective experiences? Does something need to be valued by someone before it has value? Or can certain things possess value independently of anyone's judgment?

Damn.

I only know that I value certain people. I value certain experiences, and I take pride in certain achievements. They somehow matter to me.

And the fact that they won't last forever doesn't erase the significance they hold for me. There are still things I want to keep experiencing, protecting, and working toward.

Guys, pack it up. I am incapable of answering any of these questions actually. 😂

I don't think I'll have to settle every question before I eat my cereal tomorrow, though.

So.

At the age of 23.

After all this overthinking … I still have no clue about what life ultimately means. And knowing that this life could end at any moment doesn't make the question any easier.

But not knowing doesn't feel like such a bad state of mind.

Anyway,

Still got stuff to do.
